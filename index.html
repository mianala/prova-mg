PROVA‑MG: A Probabilistic Routing Framework for Low‑Resource Malagasy Speech Recognition

A novel, unified methodology for Automatic Speech Recognition that aggregates heterogeneous foundation models, typological priors, and a large licensed unlabeled corpus under explicit calibration and explicit constraints — with a complete validation plan to prove that it works.

Abstract

Summary

Malagasy is nominally covered by every modern multilingual ASR release, yet zero‑shot quality is poor and no single foundation model, supervision regime, or adaptation trick lifts it on its own.

PROVA‑MG — Probabilistic Routing Of Variational Alignments for Malagasy — treats low‑resource ASR as a probabilistic system that aggregates heterogeneous evidence. Three foundation models (Whisper‑large‑v3, MMS, Wav2Vec2‑BERT) are kept as distinct posteriors and their disagreement is used as a routing signal; an Austronesian typological prior constrains decoding where a Malagasy‑only language model cannot; iterative pseudo‑labeling is formalized as constrained variational EM with explicit non‑collapse conditions; dialect, register, and code‑switching are unified as latent variables; and a calibration head is trained on the small supervised set to make every confidence feeding into the system trustworthy.

The seven chapters below carry the argument: the state of the art and the open gaps, the five PROVA‑MG components and their positioning, how the existing license‑first pipeline is absorbed, the risks, and the ordered validation checklist that proves each claim.
