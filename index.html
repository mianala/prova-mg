PROVA‑MG: A Probabilistic Routing Framework for Low‑Resource Malagasy Speech Recognition

A unified methodology for Automatic Speech Recognition that aggregates heterogeneous foundation models, typological priors, and a large licensed unlabeled corpus under explicit calibration and explicit constraints, together with the validation procedure that supports its claims.

Abstract

Summary

Malagasy is nominally covered by every modern multilingual ASR release, yet zero‑shot quality is poor and no single foundation model, supervision regime, or adaptation technique improves it on its own.

PROVA‑MG — Probabilistic Routing Of Variational Alignments for Malagasy — treats low‑resource ASR as a probabilistic system that aggregates heterogeneous evidence. Three foundation models (Whisper‑large‑v3, MMS, Wav2Vec2‑BERT) are retained as distinct posteriors and their disagreement is used as a routing signal; an Austronesian typological prior constrains decoding where a Malagasy‑only language model cannot; iterative pseudo‑labeling is formalized as a constrained variational EM procedure with explicit non‑collapse conditions; dialect, register, and code‑switching are unified as latent variables; and a calibration head, trained on the small supervised set, ensures that every confidence feeding into the system is trustworthy.

The four chapters below carry the argument: the foundational state of the art, the multilingual and low‑resource state of the art together with the open methodological gaps, the PROVA‑MG methodology and its integration with the existing data pipeline, and the validation procedure, expected applications, and limitations.

Contents

  1. State of the art (I): foundations of speech recognition Classical HMM‑GMM, hybrid DNN‑HMM, end‑to‑end objectives, and self‑supervised representations.
  2. State of the art (II): low‑resource and multilingual ASR Cross‑lingual SSL, weak supervision, adaptation, pseudo‑labeling, speech‑LLMs, and the open methodological gaps.
  3. Methodology: the PROVA‑MG framework Five interlocking components — co‑inference, typological priors, constrained EM, latent decomposition, calibration — and their integration with the existing pipeline.
  4. Results, application and discussion Positioning with respect to prior art, publication plan, ordered validation procedure with measurable gates, and limitations.
