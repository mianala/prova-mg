Results, application and discussion

This chapter situates each component of PROVA‑MG with respect to the closest prior art, presents the planned validation procedure as an ordered sequence of phases with measurable gates, describes the intended applications, and discusses limitations.

Part Four — A

Positioning with respect to prior art

Each component of PROVA‑MG draws on existing literature; the contribution lies in the specific formulation of each component and in their integration. The following table summarizes the nearest prior art and the elements that are new in the present work.

ComponentNearest prior artContribution of the present work
3.1 Co‑inferenceEnsemble distillation, DistilWhisperTeachers treated as distinct posteriors with informative disagreement; routing rather than averaging.
3.2 Typological priorsLanguage‑family adapters in low‑resource machine translationExplicit Bayesian prior on phonotactics integrated into the decoding probability.
3.3 Constrained EMslimIPL, Kaizen, Noisy Student TrainingFormal convergence and non‑collapse conditions in constrained variational form.
3.4 Latent decompositionDialect adapters for Arabic and ChineseJoint inference of dialect, register, and code‑switching within a single generative model.
3.5 CalibrationPost‑hoc calibration in classificationCalibration that generalizes from a small supervised set to a larger, distributionally shifted unlabeled set.

The integration of the five components is itself a contribution: the calibrated confidence (3.5), the agreement signal (3.1), and the constraint set (3.3) are shared across components, so that they reinforce one another rather than operating in isolation.

Validation plan: phases, tasks, and gates

The validation procedure is decomposed into eight phases. Each phase terminates with a gate, a measurable result that must hold before the next phase begins. Items can be marked as they are completed; progress is persisted automatically.

Validation progress 0 / 0 complete
P0

Foundations, infrastructure, and evaluation harness

A reliable measuring instrument is the precondition for every subsequent phase.

Gate 0   Baselines reproduce within a small margin of published WER, and the harness produces stable confidence intervals on a repeated run.
P1

Data substrate (license-first pipeline)

PROVA-MG consumes a gold supervised set and a large licensed unlabeled set; the pipeline produces both.

Gate 1   A licensed unlabeled pool sufficient for pseudo-labeling exists, and the supervised set is sufficient to train a routing function and a calibration head.
P2

Component 3.1 — Heterogeneous foundation-model co-inference

Show that routing across distinct teachers outperforms any single teacher, averaging, and voting.

Gate 2   Routing yields a statistically significant WER reduction over the best single teacher and over averaging, on at least two test sets. Otherwise, the disagreement signal is not informative and Component 3.1 must be revised.
P3

Component 3.5 — Confidence calibration

Built early because both 3.1 routing and 3.3 constraints consume calibrated confidence.

Gate 3   Calibrated confidence has substantially lower ECE than raw confidence and improves routing decisions; the calibrator generalizes from the supervised set to the unlabeled distribution.
P4

Component 3.2 — Typologically informed priors

Show that an Austronesian family prior constrains decoding where a Malagasy-only language model cannot.

Gate 4   Either the typological prior produces a measurable, localized WER gain (component confirmed), or it does not (component recorded as a documented negative result and dropped from the load-bearing path).
P5

Component 3.3 — Constrained variational EM

The theoretically heaviest component. Establish convergence, non-collapse, and the contribution of each constraint.

Gate 5   The constrained loop improves WER monotonically (or near-monotonically) across iterations, the unconstrained loop demonstrably collapses, and each constraint has a measurable effect.
P6

Component 3.4 — Latent dialect, register, code-switching

Show that joint inference of the latent variables outperforms treating them as separate subproblems.

Gate 6   Joint inference reduces WER on under-represented dialects and code-switched speech without degrading the majority dialect.
P7

Full-system validation and the thesis claim

Show that the integrated system outperforms every subset of its components and every published baseline.

Gate 7 (thesis claim)   The integrated PROVA-MG system significantly outperforms every ablation and every baseline on independent, frozen, dialect-stratified test sets, and the gain is attributable to the integration rather than to any single component alone.
Part Four — C

Discussion: limitations and mitigations

The methodology presents several limitations that condition the interpretation of its expected results. They are listed below together with their mitigations.

  • Scope. Five components constitute an ambitious programme. A defensible minimal thesis comprises Components 3.1, 3.3, and 3.5; Components 3.2 and 3.4 may be reported as future work if time becomes insufficient.
  • Theoretical depth. Components 3.3 and 3.5 require non‑trivial variational inference, EM convergence, and calibration theory. Adequate probabilistic supervision is therefore necessary; if it is unavailable, Component 3.3 is restricted to empirical claims supported by proof sketches.
  • Empirical uncertainty. The typological prior of Component 3.2 may fail to reduce WER if the Malagasy language model is sufficiently strong. The corresponding ablation is therefore essential, and a negative result is itself a publishable outcome.
  • Compute. Operating three teachers at scale is costly. Distillation of the co‑inference into a single student model after corpus construction is therefore planned; teachers are required at training time but not at deployment.
  • Priority of novelty. Early publication is preferred. The SIGUL submission anchors priority on the data substrate, and an early Interspeech submission on Component 3.1 anchors priority on the methodological contribution.
