The novel methodology: PROVA‑MG

Five interlocking components — co‑inference, typological priors, constrained variational EM, latent‑variable decomposition, and confidence calibration. The thesis formalizes each individually, then shows their integration beats any subset.

Part Three

Five interlocking components

Working name: PROVA‑MG, for Probabilistic Routing Of Variational Alignments for Malagasy.

Component 3.1

Heterogeneous Foundation Model Co‑Inference

Whisper‑large‑v3, MMS, and Wav2Vec2‑BERT are treated not as competing baselines but as three approximate posteriors over the transcript. Each encodes different inductive biases (weak supervision, religious‑text alignment, self‑supervision), so their disagreement on a Malagasy segment is informative.

For each unlabeled segment x we compute approximate posteriors and define a learned routing function:

r : ( q_W(y|x), q_M(y|x), q_B(y|x) ) → { train_high_confidence , route_to_human , hold_in_reserve , discard }

The routing function is trained on the small supervised set, using the agreement structure as features. We route, we do not average: averaging is Bayes‑optimal only when teachers are mutually calibrated, and these are not.

Component 3.2

Typologically Informed Bayesian Priors

Malagasy belongs to the Malayo‑Polynesian subgroup; its phonotactics and morphology overlap measurably with Indonesian, Malay, Cebuano, and Tagalog, which have far more data. We train a phonotactic and morphotactic prior on Austronesian text and incorporate it into decoding via a calibrated mixture:

P(y|x) ∝ P_acoustic(x|y) · P_LM_MG(y)^α · P_typ(y)^β

The hypothesis: this prior regularizes regions of the hypothesis space that a Malagasy‑only language model cannot constrain because of data sparsity, but a family‑level prior can.

Component 3.3

Constrained Variational EM for Iterative Pseudo‑Labeling

On the extended dataset D = D_sup union D_unsup, parameters θ and latent transcripts are inferred jointly:

E‑step:  q*(ỹ|x) = argmin_q KL( q || p_θ(ỹ|x) ) subject to constraints C M‑step:  θ ← argmax_θ E_q*[ log p_θ(y_sup, ỹ_unsup | x) ] + λ R(θ)

The constraints C are the novelty: a confidence floor, a KL divergence bound on drift between successive iterations, an agreement constraint from 3.1, and a typological consistency constraint from 3.2. Under these constraints the thesis derives convergence and non‑collapse conditions that the existing empirical literature does not articulate.

Component 3.4

Latent Variable Decomposition: Dialect, Register, Code‑Switching

The generation process is decomposed into a graphical model with explicit latent variables:

x → ( d , r , c , y ) d = dialect   r = register   c = per‑segment code‑switching language identity

The acoustic model factorizes as p(x|y,d,r), the language model as p(y|d,c,r). Dialect‑specific adapters condition on d, register parameters on r, code‑switched segments route through a French acoustic model. Threads usually treated as separate subproblems become one generative model admitting joint inference.

Component 3.5

Linguistically Informed Confidence Calibration

ASR models are overconfident; mis‑calibrated confidences sabotage the routing of 3.1 and the constraints of 3.3. A small post‑hoc calibration model (temperature scaling or isotonic regression) is trained on the supervised set, with features including model entropy, three‑way teacher agreement, typological likelihood, segment duration, voice‑activity confidence, and dialect‑posterior entropy. The calibrated confidence is what every downstream constraint actually consumes.

The unifying claim Low‑resource ASR is best approached not as one model on one objective with one source of supervision, but as a probabilistic system that aggregates heterogeneous evidence under explicit calibration and explicit constraints. Malagasy is the empirical demonstration.
