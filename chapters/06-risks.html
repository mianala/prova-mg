Risks and honesty

Where the methodology can fail, where the scope can be cut, and where priority should be anchored.

Part Six

Known risks and mitigations

  • Scope. Five components is ambitious. A defensible thesis is 3.1 + 3.3 + 3.5, with 3.2 and 3.4 as future work if time runs short.
  • Theoretical depth. Components 3.3 and 3.5 need honest variational inference, EM convergence, and calibration theory. Strong probabilistic supervision (ideally a co‑director) is advisable; if unavailable, scope 3.3 to empirical claims with sketched proofs.
  • Empirical uncertainty. Typological priors (3.2) may not move WER if the Malagasy LM is strong. The ablation is essential, and a negative result is itself publishable.
  • Compute. Three teachers at scale is expensive. Mitigation: distill the co‑inference into a single student once the corpus exists; teachers are needed at training time, not deployment.
  • Novelty contestability. Publish early. SIGUL anchors priority on data; a fast Interspeech submission on 3.1 anchors priority on methodology.
