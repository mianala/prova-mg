The existing methodological landscape

The question of novelty is not "which algorithm" but "at which layer of the stack does the contribution live, and what does it claim". The field is best read as ten layers, each largely settled.

Part One

Ten layers of the ASR stack

1.1 The classical layer

The HMM‑GMM paradigm modeled speech as a hidden Markov chain emitting Gaussian mixtures over short‑frame features (MFCC, PLP). It introduced the decomposition P(W|X) proportional to P(X|W) P(W) that still underlies any probabilistic reading of ASR, and weighted finite‑state transducer decoding that remains the formal substrate of beam search. No thesis can be "about" this layer today, but any thesis that ignores it is theoretically thin.

1.2 The hybrid layer (DNN‑HMM, 2009 to 2016)

Deep neural networks replaced the Gaussian mixture as the emission model while the decoder, lexicon, and language model stayed external. Kaldi chain models, TDNN‑F, and lattice‑free MMI live here. This regime is engineering‑heavy and unforgiving on data.

1.3 The end‑to‑end layer (2014 to 2020)

Three objectives still structure the field. CTC (Graves et al. 2006) marginalizes over all monotonic alignments and is the dominant choice when data is scarce because it converges fast with few hyperparameters. Attention encoder‑decoder (Listen, Attend and Spell, Chan et al. 2016) drops the monotonic assumption and is more expressive but more data‑hungry. RNN‑Transducer (Graves 2012) reintroduces monotonic alignment with an internal predictor and is the production choice for streaming. Conformer (Gulati et al. 2020) is the encoder that combines convolution for local patterns with self‑attention for long range context.

1.4 The self‑supervised representation layer (2018 to 2022)

Wav2Vec 2.0 (Baevski et al. 2020) introduced contrastive self‑supervised pre‑training on unlabeled audio; HuBERT (Hsu et al. 2021) replaced the contrastive term with masked prediction over discretized targets; WavLM added denoising; data2vec unified modalities under self‑distillation. Fine‑tuned with a small supervised head, these representations often match models trained from scratch on far more data.

1.5 The cross‑lingual self‑supervised layer (2020 to 2024)

XLSR‑53 and XLS‑R (Babu et al. 2022, 436,000 hours, 128 languages), MMS (Pratap et al. 2023, over 1,100 languages), and Wav2Vec2‑BERT (Seamless Communication 2024, roughly 4.5 million hours, 143 languages) produce universal acoustic representations. Malagasy is nominally covered by all of them; in practice the zero‑shot quality is poor.

1.6 The large‑scale weak supervision layer (2022 to present)

Whisper (Radford et al. 2023) trained on 680,000 hours of audio paired with weakly supervised web transcripts. USM, Canary, OWSM, and OLMoASR are variants and open reimplementations. Whisper‑large‑v3 currently "knows the most" about Malagasy of any open release, which is why the SIGUL pipeline uses it for first‑pass pseudo‑labels.

1.7 The parameter‑efficient adaptation layer

LoRA, adapters, BitFit, and prompt tuning specialize large encoders by training a small fraction of parameters. For low‑resource ASR this matters both for GPU budget and for reducing overfitting on small supervised sets.

1.8 The semi‑supervised and pseudo‑labeling layer

slimIPL, Kaizen, and Noisy Student Training share one skeleton: train a seed model, transcribe a large unlabeled set, filter by confidence, retrain on the union, repeat. The variations concern filtering, teacher update, and collapse prevention. The procedure works empirically but its conditions of success are not formally characterized.

1.9 The speech‑LLM integration layer (2023 to present)

Recent work feeds speech to a large language model, either as discrete tokens or through a learned projection (SALMONN, Qwen‑Audio). Quality on well‑resourced languages rivals dedicated ASR; on low‑resource languages the bottleneck shifts to the LLM's text coverage.

1.10 The data and licensing layer

This is the layer the existing SIGUL draft occupies: license‑first data construction, segment‑level traceability, datasheets, reproducibility. It is genuinely contributive, but it is a methodology for constructing a corpus, not for modeling speech. Necessary but not sufficient for a thesis whose center is "neural and probabilistic approaches".
