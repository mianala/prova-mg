Where the gaps actually are

Most low‑resource ASR moves are already "taken" at thesis scale. A few are genuinely under‑explored — and they are the moves that PROVA‑MG occupies.

Part Two

What is taken, and what is genuinely open

Several methods are "taken", in the sense that completed theses or near‑thesis bodies of work already exist for them: continued self‑supervised pre‑training for a single low‑resource language, pseudo‑labeling with confidence filtering, LoRA and adapter cross‑lingual fine‑tuning, dialect‑conditioned adapters, and code‑switched ASR. Doing any of these for Malagasy would be linguistically new but not methodologically new.

What is genuinely under‑explored at thesis scale:

  • The principled probabilistic integration of multiple heterogeneous foundation models treated as distinct posteriors, with their disagreement structure used as a routing signal rather than discarded.
  • The injection of typological priors from a known language family as Bayesian priors that constrain the hypothesis space, rather than as soft cues for data augmentation.
  • The formal treatment of iterative pseudo‑labeling as a constrained variational EM procedure with explicit safeguards against model collapse, with convergence analysis.
  • The unification of dialect, register, and code‑switching as latent variables in one graphical model with explicit conditional independence structure.
The thesis claim Each item above is a thinkable Interspeech paper. Their combination, applied coherently to one under‑resourced language with a corpus you built yourself under a license‑first regime, is a thinkable PhD thesis.
