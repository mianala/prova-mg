Casual note

So, what’s the new methodology?

Instead of training one model on the little Malagasy data we have, I treat ASR as a system: three pre‑trained models “vote”, related Austronesian languages fill the gaps, and I only trust labels that survive a few sanity checks.

The big multilingual models — Whisper, MMS, Wav2Vec2‑BERT — nominally support Malagasy, but the transcripts are rough. We don’t have enough clean labelled audio to train from scratch, and fine‑tuning one model alone doesn’t fix it.

1. Use the disagreement

I run all three foundation models on the same audio. When they agree, the transcript is probably right; when they don’t, that’s a signal. A small classifier decides per segment: keep, send to a human, hold, or discard.

2. Borrow from cousin languages

Malagasy is Austronesian. Indonesian, Malay, Tagalog and Cebuano have far more text. I train a small language model on all of them and use it as a soft prior during decoding — it nudges the system toward transcripts that look Austronesian even when the Malagasy‑only LM has never seen the word.

3. Pseudo‑label, but carefully

I let the model transcribe unlabeled audio and retrain on its own transcripts, but I refuse any transcript that doesn’t pass four checks: calibrated confidence high enough, at least two of three teachers agree, the typological prior accepts it, and the model isn’t drifting too fast iteration to iteration. That keeps the loop from collapsing.

4. Dialect, register, and code‑switching are part of the model

Merina, Betsimisaraka, Sakalava, Antandroy — different sounds, different vocab. People also switch into French mid‑sentence. The model jointly infers dialect, register, and which segments are French, alongside the transcript. Each helps the others.

5. Make the confidence numbers honest

ASR models always claim 95% confidence. A small calibration head, trained on the labelled set, maps raw scores to real probabilities. Once those numbers are trustworthy, the routing and the pseudo‑label filter actually work.

None of the ingredients are new on their own. The claim is the combination: the five pieces feed each other — the calibrator feeds the router and the EM constraints, the typological prior and the teacher agreement each feed a constraint, the latent adapters are used by everything else. Pull one out and the rest get weaker.

I also write down the four conditions under which the pseudo‑label loop provably doesn’t collapse, rather than just reporting that it happened to work.

It isn’t magic. The calibrator and the router both need the small supervised set, so it doesn’t bootstrap from zero. Running three foundation models on a large unlabeled pool dominates the compute budget. And until the experiments in Chapter IV are run, the numbers are expected, not measured.
